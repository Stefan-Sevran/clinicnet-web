1 · Executive Summary

Why this research matters

Dental clinics across the Philippines invest heavily to attract patient attention through Google Maps, Facebook, SEO, advertising, referrals, and social media. This study focused on what happens after that interest already exists: the final journey from “I want to book” to an actual appointment.

The study suggests that meaningful patient leakage can occur inside that final booking journey itself — through delayed replies, missed calls, confusing paths, broken redirects, channel overload, and communication that does not reassure the patient at the moment they are ready to act.

The mobile-first field study reviewed patient journeys that included Google Maps, websites, Messenger and other communication channels, booking flows, response behavior, and mobile usability.

Core finding

High-intent patients can be lost because of friction inside the booking journey.

Observed friction included delayed replies, broken redirects, confusing booking flows, overloaded channel choices, impersonal automation, missed calls, and emotional trust friction — all at a point where the patient was already attempting to move closer to a booking.

Selected findings

01

Late replies can cost high-intent inquiries

In a Cebu subset of 30 Messenger tests described in the preliminary study, no human response arrived within five hours. The observation illustrates how a motivated patient may move on before the clinic ever re-engages.

02

Google Maps can send patients into weak funnels

The study observed clinic contact paths leading to broken Facebook redirects, confusing sites, outdated booking pages, or other destinations that made the next step harder instead of easier.

03

More channels do not automatically mean less friction

When patients are simultaneously presented with Messenger, WhatsApp, Viber, SMS, calls, forms, email, and websites, accessibility can turn into uncertainty about which path will actually receive a fast response.

04

Automation needs reassurance

Healthcare booking is often emotional. Automated responses can help with speed, but the study highlighted the value of warmth, clarity, reassurance, and human judgment when conversations become uncertain or sensitive.

05

Missed calls and abandoned journeys are recoverable opportunities

Not every missed contact becomes a recovered booking, but rapid follow-up can create another chance to help a patient who otherwise may simply contact the next clinic.

What stronger funnels had in common

The future conversion leaders in healthcare may not simply be the clinics with the largest advertising budgets, but the clinics creating the fastest, warmest, simplest, and most reassuring patient booking experiences.
