THE EDEN STANDARD™

A patient-access benchmark for modern clinics.

The Eden Standard measures how reliably a clinic turns patient interest into a clear, fast, reassuring path to care — across messages, calls, booking, follow-up, and recovery.

THE FRAMEWORK

Five systems decide whether interest becomes a booking.

Every Eden Score™ is built from five practical dimensions. Each one can be improved independently, and together they describe the clinic’s patient-access system.

01⚡

Response Speed

How quickly a patient receives a useful first reply — not merely an acknowledgement.

02🗓️

Booking Clarity

How easy it is to understand the next step and actually secure an appointment.

03♡

Trust & Comfort

How credible, human, reassuring, and patient-friendly the experience feels.

04↗

Follow-up Systems

How consistently the clinic continues guiding a patient after first contact.

05☎

Recovery Systems

How effectively missed calls, delayed inquiries, and abandoned bookings are recovered.

HOW THE STANDARD IS USED

Audit first. Improve second. Certification only when earned.

1

Clinic Audit

Mia collects the clinic context. Eden reviews visible patient pathways, booking friction, and recovery gaps.

2

Evidence-backed Score

The clinic receives its Eden Score™, findings, evidence, quick wins, and estimated recovery opportunity.

3

Optional Improvement

The clinic can implement changes itself or use Eden for web chat, missed-call recovery, follow-up, and AI + Human Team support.

4

Certification

Certification is separate from the audit. It should represent verified performance against formal Eden Standard criteria.

EDEN STANDARD CERTIFICATION

Certification should mean something.

We are deliberately keeping certification stricter than a marketing badge. As the methodology matures, certified clinics will need to meet published thresholds and provide verifiable evidence.

✓ Published qualification thresholds

✓ Evidence-backed patient-access checks

✓ Reassessment when systems materially change

✓ Clear distinction between “audited” and “certified”
